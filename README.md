# Crash Course Analytics

**Project:** Who pays the highest price on San Diego Streets?

## About the project

This project looks at serious and fatal injuries in traffic collisions recorded by the San Diego Police Department between 2015 and 2025. It covers city streets only, not the freeways patrolled by CHP.

We ask whether collision frequency and injury severity differ by:

- road-user group
- time
- geographic area
- collision characteristics

Pedestrians, bicyclists and motorcyclists get particular attention as vulnerable road users.

## Approach

- **Counting:** We keep collision-level and person-level records separate, so a collision involving several people is not counted more than once.
- **Severity:** We measure injury severity as a rate per person involved, not as raw counts. This keeps groups with different exposure comparable.
- **Descriptive analysis:** We look at annual trends and at differences by road-user role, hour, day of week, police beat, hit-and-run status, vehicle type and primary violation.
- **COVID-19:** We treat patterns during 2020 separately when reading long-term trends.
- **Regression:** We estimate adjusted associations between participant and collision characteristics and the probability of serious or fatal injury.

## Website

The interactive website follows the structure of the written report. It uses trend charts, temporal heatmaps, road-user comparisons and police-beat maps to show the findings in space and time. Separating areas with high collision frequency from areas with high injury severity supports clearer traffic-safety recommendations.
