What kind of places
have more severe crashes?

A correlation is a number from −1 to +1 that shows whether two things rise and fall together. Here we compare each police beat’s serious injury rate with 44 characteristics, from our collision data and from the Census, SANDAG, the City of San Diego and SDPD crime records.

  5. 5. How we know

  • Red, or +

    Both rise together: beats with more of one tend to have more of the other.

  • Blue, or −

    Opposite: as one rises, the other tends to fall.

  • Not cause and effect

    These are patterns between places, not proof that one thing causes the other.

What this chapter tells us

  • Denser beats have a lower share of severe injuries. This is the strongest link we found (−0.35).
  • Larger, spread out beats, and beats with more unsafe speed crashes, have a higher share.
  • Most other characteristics, like income, poverty and traffic volume, show no clear link.

The next question

Places explain only part of it, and many of these characteristics overlap. To see what matters on its own, we test everything together.
