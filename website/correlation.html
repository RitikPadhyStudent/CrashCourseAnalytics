Chapter 3 of 5 · The places

What kind of places have more severe crashes?

A correlation is a number from −1 to +1 that shows whether two things rise and fall together across police beats. Here we compare each beat's serious/fatal injury rate with 44 characteristics, from our collision data and from outside sources (Census, SANDAG roads, City of San Diego, SDPD crime data). Switch to the heatmap to see every pair at once.

  5. 5How we know
  • Red or +Both rise together: beats with more of one tend to have more of the other.
  • Blue or −Opposite: as one rises, the other tends to fall.
  • Not cause and effectThese are patterns between places, not proof that one thing causes the other.

What this chapter tells us

  • Denser beats have a lower share of severe injuries. This is the strongest link we found (−0.35).
  • Larger, spread-out beats, and beats with more unsafe-speed crashes, have a higher share.
  • Most other characteristics, like income, poverty and traffic volume, show no clear link.
