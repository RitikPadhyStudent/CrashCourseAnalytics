Chapter 4 of 5 · Putting it together

So who pays the highest price?

A regression looks at many factors together and works out how much each one matters on its own, after the others are held constant. Each row below compares people in otherwise similar crashes. Hover a row for what it means in plain words.

  4. 4Putting it together
  5. 5How we know
  • The 1× lineNo difference. A dot on the line means that factor does not change the risk.
  • The dotHow much the odds change. 2× means double the odds; 0.5× means half.
  • The barThe range the true value likely falls in. If it crosses 1×, the effect could be chance.

What this chapter tells us

  • Even in otherwise similar crashes, pedestrians have 9.3 times, motorcyclists 8.6 times and bicyclists 4.9 times the odds of a driver of being seriously hurt or killed.
  • DUI crashes raise the odds 2.5 times, crashes at night 2.1 times, and crashes involving a truck or bus 1.6 times.
  • The place still matters a little: in denser beats the odds are slightly lower.
  • The answer: people outside a car pay the highest price on San Diego streets, and most of all at night and when a driver is drunk.
